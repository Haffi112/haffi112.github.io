About

I am an Associate Professor of Computer Science at the University of Iceland, where since 2026 I have also been Head of the Faculty of Industrial Engineering, Mechanical Engineering and Computer Science. I am a Principal Scientist at Amgen deCODE and a co-founder of KatlaCode. My group works on natural-language processing for low-resource Germanic languages, primarily Icelandic and Faroese, with adjacent work on computer vision for the natural sciences, clinical AI, and human genetics.

I trained at ETH Zurich (PhD 2017) in mathematical models of neural systems. After a postdoc in systems neuroscience and two years as a data scientist in banking, I joined the University of Iceland in 2020 to build a group around language models and datasets for under-resourced languages. The group currently has three PhD students. I previously co-founded Prescriby (2019–2023).

Talking about AI

A growing part of my work happens outside papers. I use AI every day and am regularly startled by how fast it moves, so I write op-eds, give talks to anyone from nurses to central bankers, and turn up on radio and television to discuss what it means for Iceland. A list of interviews and articles is on the research page.

In October 2026 I made Could AI destroy us? (Gæti gervigreindin grandað okkur?), seven short films that each retell one serious argument about how AI could go badly wrong. They are on YouTube in Icelandic and English, and on TikTok. I also wrote up how they were made.

Beyond research

I came to research through an interest in mathematics and computer science. A long detour into neuroscience followed, asking how small, almost identical circuits give rise to the complexity of cortex. I don't expect to find the "canonical microcircuit" in my career, but the mathematical foundations laid in those early years stayed with me, and they shape how I work on language and AI today. There's an Icelandic saying for it: lengi býr að fyrstu gerð, what is first shaped lasts long.

For journalists

I am happy to talk to the media about AI and society, large language models, AI in education, AI risk and safety, and language technology for Icelandic. Email is the quickest way to reach me: hafsteinne@hi.is. Earlier interviews and articles are on the research page.

Short bio

Hafsteinn Einarsson is an associate professor of computer science and Head of the Faculty of Industrial Engineering, Mechanical Engineering and Computer Science at the University of Iceland, and a principal scientist at Amgen deCODE. His research covers language technology for Icelandic, the evaluation and safety of AI systems, and machine learning in science and medicine.
